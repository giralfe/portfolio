\section{Physics and research}
\begin{itemize}
\proj{Flatland: 2D multibody physics engine (MSc thesis)}{2026--, C++17 / Python}{discrete-time Lagrangian mechanics: joints, ropes, motors and frictional contact as exact constraints, elasticity as compliant constraints, one Spook stepper and one boxed-LCP solve. I rebuilt the research library around an entity component system so bodies of any degree-of-freedom width feed one solver, in a C++17 core driven from Python. Measured: a pendulum holds its constraint to $1.6\times10^{-5}$\,m, energy stays within 0.25\% over 20\,000 steps, 250 disks step in 0.74\,ms. A symbolic verifier re-derives the algebra and fails the build if theory and code disagree.}
\proj{HexForge: GPU particle-physics engine}{2024--, C++20 / CUDA / OpenGL}{fluids (Position Based Fluids), cloth (XPBD), sand and rigid bodies in one CUDA kernel suite, with screen-space fluid rendering. Spacing, kernel radius, substep bound and damping derived from physical inputs; each kernel traced to its paper.}
\proj{Particletoys: particle, cloth and fluid simulation}{2026, C++, team of 3, TU/e}{my parts: a constraint solver (Lagrange multipliers, Baumgarte, matrix-free conjugate gradient); a measured comparison of four integrators whose stable step matched a hand estimate from the cloth's stiffness; cloth; and on Stam's fluid solver a level-set free surface, two-way rigid-body coupling, buoyancy and temperature.}
\proj{scrye: electron-microscopy simulation}{2026, Python / NumPy}{Monte Carlo electron transport (backscatter, secondaries, characteristic X-rays, energy loss, knock-on damage) and a Cowley--Moodie multislice engine for HRTEM, STEM and diffraction.}
\proj{MochaNES: NES emulator}{2025--, Java}{CPU, PPU, APU and six mappers, measured against blargg's hardware test ROMs (35 of 51 pass), with a physically motivated CRT model and a browser build.}
\end{itemize}
